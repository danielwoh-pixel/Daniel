Top 10 Brands Served · July 2026

223 unique candidates (66.4%) have experience at one or more Top 10 brands. adidas remains the largest talent ecosystem with 101 candidates (30.1%), ahead of Nike (33) and Puma (28).

Top 10 Nationalities · July 2026

American designers lead at 108 (32.1%), followed by Korean (46) and Chinese (42). The Top 10 nationalities represent 93.2% of the pool (313 of 336).

Preferred Work Destinations · July 2026

China received 126 destination selections (37.5%) and Japan 112 (33.3%), forming a dual-center structure for design talent mobility in Asia.

Cross-Border Flows · July 2026

Of 238 cross-border destination selections, US→Japan (33) and Korea→China (29) lead. 187 candidates (55.7%) have at least one cross-border country preference.

Salary Distribution · July 2026

Based on 102 valid salary records (30.4%). The US leads among larger samples with a median of approximately RMB 1.59M; China, Germany and Korea offer more competitive salary structures.

Market Watch · July 2026

From Brand Prestige to Capability

How hiring priorities are evolving in footwear and apparel design.

Latest Talent Intelligence July 2026 · Top Brands Served

For many years, recruitment has been shaped by brand reputation — designers from Nike, adidas, and Puma have traditionally enjoyed significant market advantage. Today, employers increasingly emphasize what designers can deliver: cross-category experience, global product creation, consumer insight, and commercial execution. Brand background remains important, but capability, adaptability, and international experience are becoming the primary measures of competitiveness.
