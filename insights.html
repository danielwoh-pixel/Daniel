Global Footwear & Apparel Design Talent Intelligence

The July database covers 336 global footwear and apparel design professionals who are open to relevant opportunities. Thirteen candidates were added this month, expanding the database by 4.0%. Talent Analytics · July 2026

Top 10 Brands Served

A total of 101 candidates have adidas experience, representing 30.1% of the database and five more than in June. There are 223 unique candidates with experience at one or more Top 10 brands, equal to 66.4% of the database. Nike (33), Puma (28) and The North Face (27) form critical secondary pools. Anta (10), FILA (9) and Li-Ning (9) reflect growing visibility of Asian sports brands.

Talent Analytics · July 2026

Top 10 Nationalities

The database includes 108 American candidates (32.1%), reinforcing the US position as a major training ground for sports, outdoor and lifestyle design talent. Korean representation rose to 46, Chinese to 42, and Japanese to 23 — together contributing 11 of the 13 July additions. The Top 10 nationalities account for 313 candidates, or 93.2% of the pool.

Talent Analytics · July 2026

Preferred Work Destinations

China was selected by 126 candidates (37.5% of the database) — ten more than in June and the strongest monthly increase among all destinations. Japan received 112 selections (33.3%). A total of 217 candidates selected at least one of China, Japan or Korea, while the US and Europe still received 74 and 36 selections respectively. Candidate mobility is clearly multi-destination rather than market-exclusive.

Talent Analytics · July 2026

Cross-border Design Talent Flows

Of 238 cross-border destination selections, China received 95 and Japan 94 — together 79.4% of all cross-border selections. US→Japan ranks first with 33 selections, followed by Korea→China with 29. A total of 187 candidates (55.7%) have at least one cross-border country preference. The Top 10 flows account for 149 selections, or 62.6% of all flows.

Talent Analytics · July 2026

Salary Distribution by Current Location

Based on 102 valid salary records from 336 candidates (30.4%). The US has the highest median among markets with larger samples (approximately RMB 1.59M across 32 records), well above China (RMB 0.78M), Germany (RMB 0.68M) and Korea (RMB 0.48M). Italy and the UK show higher medians but smaller samples. Findings should be interpreted as directional rather than precise market pricing.

Market Watch · July 2026

From Interest to Onboarding

Cross-border hiring conversion for international design talent.

July data shows that 187 candidates have at least one cross-border country preference, representing 55.7% of the database. China and Japan received 95 and 94 cross-border destination selections respectively. International design talent is willing to move, but there is still a meaningful conversion gap between interest in an opportunity and a completed contract, relocation and start date.

For senior design professionals, an international move is rarely a simple salary comparison. Brand platform and compensation remain important, yet process transparency, internal decision speed and cross-functional coordination increasingly influence offer acceptance and eventual onboarding.

Hiring Intelligence · July 2026

Hiring Recommendations

Strategic priorities for brands competing in an international, ecosystem-driven design talent market.

01

Build maps around brand ecosystems and adjacent categories

Start with major ecosystems such as adidas, Nike, Puma and The North Face, while including adjacent Outdoor, Golf, Lifestyle and Luxury brands. Build a long-term pool of at least 30–50 people for each critical role.

02

Differentiate the international talent proposition for China and Japan

China can prioritize Korea, the US, the UK and Italy, while Japan can focus on the US, Germany, Korea and the UK. Role messaging should be adjusted by source market.

03

Assess transferable capability across design categories

Sportstyle, Performance and Outdoor are the three most common experience areas. Portfolio assessment should cover specialist depth, consumer insight, and the ability to transfer skills into new product contexts.

04

Treat the offer process as an international hiring capability

Prepare clear information on salary, bonus, visa, relocation, non-compete and benefits before the final interview, and set firm internal approval timelines.

05

Improve the completeness and comparability of compensation data

Only 30.4% of current records contain usable salary data. Future interviews should consistently capture base salary, bonus, equity, currency, gross or net basis and data date.
