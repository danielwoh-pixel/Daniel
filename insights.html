Talent Analytics · October 2026

Leading Brand Ecosystems

A total of 107 candidates have adidas experience, representing 27.2% of the database and about 2.9 times the Nike count. The North Face increased to 31 candidates and PUMA to 30; the other Top 10 brand counts were unchanged from the September snapshot. Nike stands at 37, Reebok and Under Armour at 15 each, New Balance at 14, and FILA, Li-Ning and Anta at 10 each. The Top 10 brands cover 245 unique candidates, or 62.2%. Another 149 come primarily from luxury, fashion, specialist outdoor and regional brands. Only two of the 11 October additions appear in the current Top 10 brand matrix.

Talent Analytics · October 2026

Global Nationality Distribution

The database contains 126 US candidates (32.0%). Korean and Chinese candidates account for 51 and 49 respectively. Of the 11 October additions, four are British, three American and three Italian, with one French candidate. The ten leading nationalities account for 369 candidates, or 93.7%. The remaining 25 candidates span 14 nationality backgrounds.

Talent Analytics · October 2026

Preferred Work Destinations

China was selected by 162 candidates (41.1%), up six from the September snapshot; three of those additional selections come from the October cohort. Japan received 122 selections (31.0%). After excluding candidates already based in each market, China and Japan record 110 and 105 cross-border selections respectively. The US, Korea and Europe received 81, 44 and 38 geographic selections. Remote, Freelance and Hybrid account for 72 selections from 71 candidates and are kept out of this geographic chart.

Talent Analytics · October 2026

Cross-border Talent Mobility

Of 255 cross-border destination selections, China received 110 and Japan 105 — together 84.3%. US→Japan ranks first with 39 selections, followed by US→China with 29. Korea→China contributes 22, Germany→Japan 21, Germany→China 17 and Italy→China 15. Among 386 candidates eligible under the 16-market current-location definition, 198 (51.3%) have at least one cross-border country preference. The top ten routes account for 181 selections, or 71.0% of all flows.

Market Watch · October 2026

Turning flexible preferences into talent options.

Engagement pathways for candidates who select Freelance, Remote or Hybrid.

Inside the October cohort, eight of the 11 additions selected at least one of Freelance, Remote or Hybrid. Across the database, 71 candidates made 72 such selections. These are candidate-database observations. They do not describe the international design market as a whole, and they do not equal relocation readiness or a lack of interest in a permanent role.

The practical question, informed by this cohort and by active international design searches, is which model the candidate wants: remote employment, a defined project, periodic travel, or a project that may convert to a permanent role. Each model needs its own budget, management rhythm, confidentiality terms and delivery scope. A bounded project can test design judgement and cross-time-zone collaboration when deliverables, reviews, fees and rights of use are set in advance. If permanent conversion is possible, role level, location, visa timing and fee treatment belong in that first conversation.

Hiring Intelligence · October 2026

Hiring Recommendations

A short preview of this month’s priorities. The full recommendation set, including suggested measures, is shared with the report.

01

Expand the talent pool beyond the Top 10

The Top 10 brands cover 245 candidates, while another 149 come from luxury, fashion, specialist outdoor and regional brands.

02

Use different source markets for China and Japan

China can prioritise the US, Korea, Germany, Italy and the UK. Japan can prioritise the US, Germany, China and Korea.

03

Create separate pathways for flexible engagement

Seventy-one candidates selected at least one of Remote, Freelance or Hybrid, including eight from the October cohort. Confirm the engagement type before a full interview or project process.

04

Compare compensation only on like-for-like role scope

The 116 compensation records cover 29.4% of the database, and market seniority structures differ. Bonus, tax, relocation and team scope need a separate check.

05

Separate new additions from field maintenance

October added 11 candidates, but compensation, mobility and location metrics also reflect updates to existing records.

Data source · October 2026

Methodology and limitations

The sample is 394 design professionals open to opportunities, across apparel, footwear, accessory, graphic, CMF and technical design. Brand, category and work-location fields are multi-response, so those percentages should not be added to 100%. Cross-border flows run from current location to a preferred work country. Same-country choices, Remote, Freelance, Hybrid and broad Europe selections are excluded. The eligible base is 386 candidates across 16 country markets.

Monthly comparisons use the published September report and this October file. Eleven records were added. Other movements can include updates to existing records, so they are database maintenance rather than a measure of the global talent market. Category completeness is 95.9%, with 16 records still pending.

Against the 26 September snapshot: total candidates moved from 383 to 394 (+11); valid salary records from 114 to 116 (+2); Luxury-category candidates from 29 to 33 (+4); candidates selecting China from 156 to 162 (+6); cross-border selections from 248 to 255 (+7).

Compensation is available for 116 records, or 29.4%. USD values are converted from cleaned RMB at RMB 7.20 per USD; the source USD reference field is excluded. The salary chart shows 107 records across eight countries. Italy and the UK are smaller samples. Japan and France have fewer than five records and are directional only.
