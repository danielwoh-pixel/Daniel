Perspective

Why VS.talent Exists

Footwear and apparel design is a specialised labour market. A creative director who has built performance footwear is not interchangeable with an apparel lead from fashion, and a CMF specialist who understands outdoor materials is not a graphic designer who has passed through a sportswear studio. Calendars, consumer problems, brand codes and team structures differ by category. Most search firms treat design as one function among many. The result is broad coverage and shallow judgment.

VS.talent exists because that gap is costly. A design leadership hire sets a product point of view for several seasons. A poor fit is not only a failed search. It is a lost line, a stalled team and a year of rework. We work only in footwear and apparel design, across sportswear, outdoor, fashion and luxury, so the same organisations, categories and career paths appear again and again. Repetition is what makes the market legible: which brand ecosystems train people, which nationalities move, which destinations are realistic, and where pay should be read as directional rather than precise.

That repetition is how market intelligence is made. Each briefing, mapping and candidate conversation records who is open, where they will work, and what experience they carry. Monthly intelligence is not an outside survey. It is the record of an active practice. A generalist database can count designers. A specialised one can tell a sportswear footwear director from a luxury apparel lead, and see when those paths transfer.

Executive search should rest on that record. A shortlist without it is a list of familiar names. A shortlist with it can show whether a role is scarce, whether China or Japan is a credible destination, and whether luxury experience will transfer to the brief. The hire, the map and the monthly view should describe the same market. That is why search at VS.talent is supported by proprietary talent intelligence, rather than conducted as a sequence of introductions.
